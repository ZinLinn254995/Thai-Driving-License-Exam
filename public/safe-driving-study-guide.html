Safe Driving Principles to Review Before Your Test

Safe driving is a continuous process of noticing hazards, judging the space and time available, and choosing actions that leave room for mistakes by other road users. For theory-test study, focus on the reason a behaviour reduces risk instead of memorizing a phrase without understanding it.

Important: This is general educational guidance, not legal advice or a replacement for a qualified instructor. Follow current Thai law, posted signs and official DLT materials.

1. Look well ahead and keep scanning

Do not focus only on the vehicle directly in front of you. Scan the road ahead, mirrors and relevant side areas, and watch for pedestrians, motorcycles, vehicles entering from side roads, changing traffic signals and sudden changes in flow. Looking ahead gives you more time to respond smoothly.

2. Leave enough space to respond

Following too closely removes the time and distance needed to react if the vehicle ahead brakes suddenly. Choose a safe gap appropriate to speed, visibility, road surface and traffic. Increase the gap when conditions are poor, and avoid treating another driver's tailgating as a reason to retaliate or brake aggressively.

3. Adjust speed to the conditions

A posted limit is not a guarantee that the same speed is safe in every situation. Rain, standing water, poor visibility, roadworks, sharp bends, heavy traffic and vulnerable road users can all require a more cautious speed. Reduce speed early and smoothly rather than waiting until a hazard is close.

4. Check before changing direction or position

Before turning, changing lanes or moving away from a kerb, signal as required, check mirrors and blind spots, and make sure the movement can be completed safely. A signal communicates intention; it does not give automatic priority or make an unsafe manoeuvre safe.

5. Remove avoidable distractions

Set navigation and audio before moving, keep attention on driving, and stop somewhere safe if you need to read or type a message. Fatigue, strong emotions and alcohol or impairing substances can reduce attention and reaction quality. Plan breaks and never drive when you are not fit to do so.

6. Protect people with less physical protection

Motorcyclists, cyclists and pedestrians can be difficult to see and are more exposed in a collision. Look carefully near junctions, crossings, parked vehicles and places where people may enter the road. Give vulnerable road users space and avoid assuming that they have seen you.

7. Plan for uncertainty

Good driving does not depend on every road user behaving perfectly. Keep escape space where possible, avoid racing to close gaps, and be ready to slow down when another person's intention is unclear. When a situation becomes unsafe, prioritise creating space and reducing risk over insisting on the right of way.

Practice with explanations

When answering a safe-driving question, identify the hazard first, then choose the response that reduces risk without creating a new danger. If an answer key suggests a manoeuvre that seems unsafe, verify it against official guidance before learning it.
