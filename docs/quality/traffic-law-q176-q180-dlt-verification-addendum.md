## Q176 — Can a mute person drive?
- Current keyed answer: Yes.
- English/Myanmar: The Myanmar translation accurately conveys the English question and answer.
- Finding: This appears plausible because inability to speak by itself does not establish inability to control a vehicle. However, the exact rule for a person who cannot speak was not separately verified in the current DLT rules reviewed. Do not confuse mute/speech disability with deafness/hearing impairment, which has specific DLT testing and vehicle-mirror requirements.
- Status: PROVISIONALLY PLAUSIBLE; verify the specific applicable DLT rule if this question is intended as an exact legal claim.

## Q177 — Prior heart surgery or myocardial ischemia
- Current keyed answer: “No, in any case.”
- Finding: Overly absolute. A history of heart surgery or myocardial ischemia does not, on its own, establish that a person can never drive. The Thai Motor Vehicle Act section 46 framework refers to a disease that a medical practitioner considers may be dangerous while driving. Individual condition, recovery, symptoms, and medical advice matter.
- Myanmar translation: Faithfully conveys the English option, but the absolute wording is the concern.
- Status: MANUAL REVIEW — likely overgeneralization; no edit made.

## Q178 — Which condition permits driving?
- Current keyed answer: Gingivitis.
- Finding: Reasonable as the least-driving-relevant condition among these choices. The other options—ischemic heart disease, epilepsy, and insulin-treated diabetes—cannot be treated as universally disqualifying solely from their labels; the question is a simplified comparative choice, not a full statement of licensing eligibility.
- Myanmar translation: “သွားဖုံးရောင်ရောဂါ” is a suitable rendering of gingivitis; the other option translations broadly align with the English.
- Status: KEY REASONABLE AMONG THESE OPTIONS; note that the question's implication about the other conditions is simplified and should not be read as a complete legal rule.

## Q179 — Loss of right arm
- Current keyed answer: May drive if able to demonstrate safe vehicle-control ability to the official.
- Finding: Supported in principle by DLT rules for applicants with arm, leg, or torso disabilities: the registrar evaluates physical ability to control the vehicle safely and may require a driving test, medical evidence, or vehicle adaptations/limitations. The exact licence and conditions depend on the person's circumstances.
- Myanmar translation: Broadly aligned with the English. “ယာဉ်အားထိန်းချုပ်နိုင်စွမ်းရှိကြောင်း တာဝန်ရှိသူအားပြသရမည်” conveys the core requirement.
- Status: SUPPORTED IN PRINCIPLE; the question is simplified because it omits possible adaptations and licence restrictions.

## Q180 — Muscle weakness
- Current keyed answer: “No, in any case.”
- Finding: Overly absolute and insufficiently specific. “Muscle weakness” covers a wide range of causes and severities. The relevant question is whether the condition makes safe vehicle control impossible or is judged medically dangerous, and whether appropriate adaptations permit safe control.
- Myanmar translation: Accurately conveys the English, but the absolute answer is the concern.
- Status: MANUAL REVIEW — likely overgeneralization; no edit made.
