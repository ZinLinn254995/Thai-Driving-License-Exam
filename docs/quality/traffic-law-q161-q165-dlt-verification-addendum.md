## Q161 — Two vehicles arrive at an intersection at the same time

- Thai stem asks which vehicle has priority when both arrive simultaneously; English and Myanmar convey the same scenario.
- Image `public/exam_images/law/1000000000297.webp` was fetched from this branch and visually inspected. Vehicle A (red) approaches from the bottom; vehicle B (blue) approaches from the left.
- Road Traffic Act section 71(2) says when vehicles arrive simultaneously and no vehicle is already in the intersection, give way to the vehicle on the driver's left, unless a major-road/minor-road arrangement applies: https://lawplayer.com/th/article/ocs-%E0%B8%880003-1B-0001/m-71
- In the diagram, B is on A's left, so keyed answer B is consistent with the left-priority rule, assuming an ordinary intersection and no overriding priority-road sign.
- **Status: Answer supported with ordinary-intersection assumptions. No data changed.**

## Q162 — Sleeping pills or anti-anxiety medication

- Thai, English and Myanmar ask whether a person who has taken sleeping pills or anti-anxiety medication should drive. The keyed answer says “Should not, in any case.”
- Thailand FDA warns that sedatives/anti-anxiety medicines such as diazepam and alprazolam may cause residual drowsiness and impair driving; drivers should heed medicine warnings and avoid driving when the medicine warns of drowsiness: https://www.fda.moph.go.th/news/2912
- The safety principle is supported, but “in any case” is more absolute than the cited guidance, which depends on the medicine, its effects and warnings.
- **Status: Safety intent supported; wording is overbroad. Keep for later approved content review. No data changed.**

## Q163 — Vision corrected by glasses

- Thai, English and Myanmar ask whether a person whose vision problem can be corrected with glasses may drive; keyed answer is “Yes.”
- DLT material describes a licence restriction requiring corrective lenses when the applicant passes the relevant vision test only with glasses/contact lenses: https://legal.dlt.go.th/repository/attachments/475_2020120301.pdf
- Corrected vision does not automatically disqualify a driver, but the person must meet the applicable vision test and comply with any corrective-lens restriction on the licence. The question omits these conditions.
- **Status: Answer broadly supported, subject to vision-test and licence conditions. No data changed.**

## Q164 — Give-way sign and dashed line

- Thai asks what the driver in the picture should do. English and Myanmar match the stem. Image `public/exam_images/law/1000000000320.webp` was fetched from this branch and visually inspected; it shows a give-way sign (`ป้ายให้ทาง`) and a dashed give-way line.
- DLT's “Driving in Thailand” manual says a yield sign means exercise caution and give way to vehicles/pedestrians on the priority road before proceeding; unlike a stop sign, it does not require a complete stop in every case: https://safedrivedlt.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Driving-EN-010468_compressed.pdf
- The keyed option says to stop and wait for the crossing-road vehicle to pass first. Yielding is correct, but a mandatory full stop is not required when the way is clear; the other options wrongly treat the dashed line/sign as permission to proceed immediately.
- **Status: Keyed option is the safest available choice, but “stop and wait” is stricter than the general give-way rule. No data changed.**

## Q165 — Correct turning technique shown in four images

- Thai asks which image shows a correct turn; English and Myanmar convey the same question.
- All four option images were fetched from this branch and visually inspected. The keyed second image (`1000000001295.webp`) shows a car positioned in a lane with straight/right-turn markings and a right-turn indicator. The other images depict a potentially unsafe/conflicting manoeuvre, a car passing a motorcycle close to the centre line, and a vehicle conflict near pedestrians.
- Road Traffic Act section 51 requires drivers to position in the appropriate lane before turning (at least 30 m before the turn in specified multi-lane cases), turn carefully, and yield to pedestrians and traffic crossing from other directions: https://lawplayer.com/th/act/ocs-%E0%B8%880003-1B-0001/section/title-3.chapter-3
- Option 2 appears to be the intended answer, but the diagram does not clearly establish road direction, exact lane markings and the full turning path sufficiently to verify the answer conclusively.
- **Status: Likely intended answer is option 2; diagram-only confirmation remains uncertain. Keep for later visual/content review. No data changed.**
